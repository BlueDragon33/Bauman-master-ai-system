# RE03 — 100-LEVEL RUSSIAN COMPETENCY LADDER

Canonical owners: RU02 + RU04 + C4

Purpose: define a product-friendly 100-level progression while preserving a multidimensional competency truth model.

IMPORTANT:
- These are INTERNAL product progression levels.
- They are not official CEFR certificates.
- Mapping to CEFR or institutional standards requires separate validated evidence.
- A learner may have uneven skill profiles.
- Level promotion is evidence-gated, not click-gated.

---

# 1. LEVEL RECORD MODEL

Each level definition should eventually contain:
- id: RL001 ... RL100;
- band;
- learner-facing title;
- target competencies;
- required prerequisites;
- content pools;
- difficulty vector;
- independent evidence gates;
- supported evidence gates;
- retention checks;
- transfer checks;
- speech/listening constraints;
- allowed support ceiling;
- promotion policy;
- remediation links;
- optional CEFR estimate with confidence, never as official equivalence.

---

# 2. TEN BANDS

## Band 1 — Sound & Grounded Meaning — RL001–RL010
Goal: Russian sounds and a small set of concrete meanings begin connecting directly.

RL001 — attend to Russian sound and speaker turn.
RL002 — distinguish a first set of highly contrasting sounds/words.
RL003 — map familiar visible objects to Russian audio.
RL004 — map simple actions to Russian commands.
RL005 — follow one-step object/action instructions.
RL006 — identify people and basic roles from context.
RL007 — understand yes/no and basic acceptance/refusal.
RL008 — imitate short high-frequency chunks with intelligible rhythm.
RL009 — respond to repeated routine prompts without translation.
RL010 — complete a tiny fully Russian scene using context only.

## Band 2 — Concrete World & Action Chunks — RL011–RL020
Goal: build a direct semantic network around objects, actions, properties and possession.

RL011 — object category expansion.
RL012 — color/size/shape grounding.
RL013 — possession and person association.
RL014 — location: in/on/under/near through action.
RL015 — movement toward/away/from/to in scenes.
RL016 — quantity and very small-number comprehension.
RL017 — simple wants/needs chunks.
RL018 — give/take/open/close/put/go/come action families.
RL019 — two-item semantic contrasts under normal clear speech.
RL020 — complete multi-turn concrete routine without native-language text.

## Band 3 — Routine Personal Interaction — RL021–RL030
Goal: function inside predictable personal and household interactions.

RL021 — greetings and leave-taking as chunks.
RL022 — name/identity exchange.
RL023 — family/people references.
RL024 — food/drink request and response.
RL025 — basic time-of-day routines.
RL026 — simple likes/dislikes.
RL027 — possession/request/permission routines.
RL028 — 2-step spoken instructions.
RL029 — short question-answer chains.
RL030 — one-minute predictable Russian-only interaction with low support.

## Band 4 — Survival & Repair — RL031–RL040
Goal: keep communication alive in common real-world situations.

RL031 — ask where something is.
RL032 — understand simple directions.
RL033 — numbers/prices in purchase context.
RL034 — transport destination and basic ticket interaction.
RL035 — dormitory/basic accommodation interaction.
RL036 — classroom/basic university request.
RL037 — request repetition and slower speech.
RL038 — say non-understanding and ask clarification.
RL039 — confirm critical information.
RL040 — complete a survival scenario including at least one repair turn.

## Band 5 — Natural Connected Speech Bridge — RL041–RL050
Goal: move from careful educational audio to ordinary native connected speech.

RL041 — recognize known chunks at normal speed.
RL042 — tolerate common vowel reduction in known words.
RL043 — recognize devoicing/assimilation effects in familiar language.
RL044 — use word stress reliably in core active vocabulary.
RL045 — follow short connected instructions without transcript.
RL046 — discriminate meaning under moderate speaker variation.
RL047 — shadow phrase rhythm without losing meaning.
RL048 — respond within reduced latency in routine dialogue.
RL049 — understand gist with one unfamiliar non-critical item.
RL050 — complete a normal-speed familiar scenario without automatic translation.

## Band 6 — Social Meaning & Narrative — RL051–RL060
Goal: understand and produce connected personal/social meaning beyond immediate commands.

RL051 — describe current activity.
RL052 — describe recent simple events.
RL053 — express plan/intention.
RL054 — compare people/objects/choices.
RL055 — explain reason using common causal patterns.
RL056 — understand short personal story.
RL057 — retell gist with known Russian.
RL058 — handle simple misunderstanding and self-correction.
RL059 — maintain several conversational turns on a familiar topic.
RL060 — produce a short spontaneous narrative with understandable sequence.

## Band 7 — Independent Daily & University Life — RL061–RL070
Goal: operate with increasing independence in Russia and a university environment.

RL061 — service/admin forms and spoken confirmation.
RL062 — university schedule/deadline discussion.
RL063 — ask a teacher/classmate for clarification.
RL064 — understand assignment instructions.
RL065 — explain a practical problem.
RL066 — phone/voice interaction without visual grounding in familiar domains.
RL067 — take useful short notes from speech.
RL068 — summarize a short informational text/audio.
RL069 — participate in small-group familiar discussion.
RL070 — resolve an unfamiliar but bounded university-life scenario using repair strategies.

## Band 8 — Academic & Technical Russian — RL071–RL080
Goal: learn and communicate technical/academic content, especially for university study.

RL071 — recognize academic classroom discourse markers.
RL072 — follow structured technical explanation with visuals.
RL073 — define a familiar technical concept in simple Russian.
RL074 — explain process/sequence.
RL075 — compare methods/options.
RL076 — understand condition/cause/result in technical speech.
RL077 — ask precise academic clarification.
RL078 — present a short technical explanation.
RL079 — read and discuss an adapted technical passage.
RL080 — complete a technical problem-discussion scenario with evidence-based explanation.

## Band 9 — Research & Professional Production — RL081–RL090
Goal: produce and defend increasingly complex academic/research meaning.

RL081 — summarize source claims accurately.
RL082 — distinguish claim, evidence and opinion.
RL083 — describe data/result trends.
RL084 — explain method and procedure.
RL085 — state limitation/uncertainty.
RL086 — connect sources without fabricating support.
RL087 — present a structured research update.
RL088 — answer follow-up questions under mild pressure.
RL089 — repair/rephrase when a technical answer is misunderstood.
RL090 — conduct a bounded research/technical discussion with sustained coherence.

## Band 10 — High-Autonomy Russian — RL091–RL100
Goal: robust independent operation across everyday, university, technical and research domains.

RL091 — follow extended native speech with selective note-taking.
RL092 — adapt register between peer, staff and academic contexts.
RL093 — handle fast multi-speaker discussion in familiar domains.
RL094 — infer meaning of some unfamiliar vocabulary from context.
RL095 — maintain accurate meaning while reformulating.
RL096 — present complex technical/research content with controlled structure.
RL097 — defend a choice/method under challenging questions.
RL098 — synthesize information across several sources or speakers.
RL099 — operate for an extended session primarily in Russian with minimal support.
RL100 — demonstrate high autonomous transfer across target life/study/research domains.

RL100 does NOT mean “native speaker”.
It means the product's highest validated autonomy target for the defined domains.

---

# 3. PROMOTION POLICY

Promotion should require a configurable combination of:
- independent comprehension;
- production;
- delayed retention;
- transfer to unseen content;
- acceptable support dependency;
- oral/listening evidence where relevant.

A learner may enter higher-level content without being globally “promoted” if diagnostics show a strong skill.

Do not lock advanced adults behind trivial content they can already prove.

---

# 4. DIAGNOSTIC ENTRY

Support placement by evidence:
- listening sample;
- speaking sample;
- reading;
- writing;
- contextual comprehension;
- repair;
- vocabulary production;
- academic/technical sample when relevant.

Diagnostic confidence must be stored.

Do not overclaim from one short test.

---

# 5. DIFFICULTY VECTOR

Each experience may vary:
- semantic novelty;
- lexical novelty;
- grammatical novelty;
- acoustic speed;
- speaker variability;
- noise;
- visual support;
- transcript availability;
- response openness;
- response latency;
- number of turns;
- memory delay;
- social pressure;
- technical abstraction.

Level progression should move this vector gradually.

---

# 6. LEVEL ≠ MASTERY

Level is a curriculum/progression convenience.

Canonical mastery remains competency/evidence based.

If listening is RL60-equivalent and speaking is RL35-equivalent, the system must preserve that asymmetry rather than hiding it behind one badge.

---

# 7. RETENTION GATE

Important competencies must survive delayed checks.

Promotion must not erase review needs.

A high level can coexist with scheduled review of older weak items.

---

# 8. TRANSFER GATE

At major milestones RL010, RL020, RL030 ... RL100, require an unseen or recombined scenario.

Memorized exact-sentence reproduction alone cannot satisfy the transfer gate.

---

# 9. PRODUCT PRESENTATION

The UI may present levels attractively:
- 1–100;
- worlds/bands;
- maps;
- milestones;
- capability cards.

But learner-facing presentation must not imply false official certification.

---

# 10. RE03 EXIT GATE

PASS when:
- all 100 levels are represented as data definitions or validated schema-ready records;
- level-to-competency relationships are explicit;
- promotion uses RU04 evidence;
- no level requires unique hardcoded UI;
- diagnostics can place learners without forcing level-by-level repetition;
- transfer and retention gates exist.
